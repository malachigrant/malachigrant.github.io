A Timeless Favorite

Classic Pound Cake

Four simple ingredients, one perfect ratio — the cake that has graced tea tables for centuries.

Serves 12 slices
Prep 20 min
Bake 60–70 min
Total ~1.5 hrs

About This Cake

Named for its original recipe — one pound each of flour, sugar, butter, and eggs — pound cake is the ultimate demonstration that simplicity, done well, beats complexity. The result is a dense, buttery, deeply satisfying cake with a golden crust and a tender crumb.

It's equally at home with morning coffee, afternoon tea, or a scoop of vanilla ice cream for dessert. Serve it plain, or dust with powdered sugar and a few fresh berries.

Ingredients

  • 2 cupsunsalted butter, softened to room temperature
  • 2 cupsgranulated sugar
  • 4large eggs, room temperature
  • 1 tspvanilla extract
  • 2 ½ cupsall-purpose flour, spooned and leveled
  • ½ tspfine salt
  • —butter & flour for the pan

Instructions

Preheat the oven to 325°F (165°C). Butter a 9×5-inch loaf pan thoroughly, then dust with flour, tapping out the excess. (For extra insurance against sticking, line the pan with parchment paper before adding flour.)

In a large bowl, cream the butter and sugar together with an electric mixer on medium-high speed for 4–5 minutes, until pale, fluffy, and significantly increased in volume. This step builds the cake's texture — don't rush it.

Add the eggs one at a time, beating well after each addition. Scrape down the sides of the bowl as needed. Stir in the vanilla.

In a separate bowl, whisk together the flour and salt. Add to the butter mixture in three additions, mixing on low speed just until incorporated after each. Overmixing here makes the cake tough.

Scrape the batter into the prepared pan and smooth the top. Bake for 60–70 minutes, until the top is golden brown and a toothpick inserted into the center comes out clean. If the top is browning too quickly, cover loosely with foil.

Let the cake cool in the pan for 20 minutes, then turn it out onto a wire rack to cool completely. Slice once fully cooled for the cleanest cuts.

Baker's Tips

  • Why 4 eggs? The classic modern ratio calls for 4 large eggs for this amount of butter and sugar. (The old "one pound each" tradition means about 8, but 4 is what reliably gives a tender, buttery crumb — not a dry or eggy one.)
  • Room temperature is key. Cold butter and eggs won't cream properly, and the batter may curdle.
  • Spoon and level the flour. Scooping packs flour into the cup and dries out the cake.
  • Low and slow. A moderate oven temperature gives an even bake and a tender crumb.
  • Make it yours. Try a lemon glaze (2 tbsp lemon juice + ½ cup powdered sugar), orange zest in the batter, or a dusting of cinnamon sugar over the top before baking.
  • Storage. Keeps well wrapped at room temperature for 3–4 days, or freeze slices for up to 2 months.
